About Thavinhal Grama Panchayat

A geographical and natural resource profile

Thavinhal Grama Panchayat is located in Wayanad district of Kerala, within the Mananthavady Block Panchayat. It is characterised by a scenic landscape of hills, valleys, streams, agricultural fields and green vegetation. The region forms part of the ecologically significant Western Ghats landscape.


Agriculture is an important economic activity in the region. Banana, pepper and ginger cultivation contribute to the agricultural landscape. Forest vegetation, bamboo, teak and rosewood are among the natural features reported in the area.


The geographical setting and natural resources of Thavinhal provide important opportunities for studying the relationship between the physical environment, biodiversity, agriculture and rural livelihoods.

Geographical Information

Location and physical characteristics

📍 Geographical Location

Thavinhal is situated in Wayanad district in the northern part of Kerala. It comes under Mananthavady Block Panchayat.

⛰️ Topography

The landscape includes elevated hills, valleys, slopes and undulating terrain. Muneeswaran Hill (Muneeswarankunnu) is a notable elevated location.

🌊 Drainage and Water Resources

The area contains streams and small watercourses. A stream originating from Makkimala joins the Kabani river system.

🌳 Natural Vegetation

The natural landscape includes tree cover, bamboo and forest vegetation. Teak and rosewood are among the trees reported in the village.

🌱 Soil and Agriculture

The agricultural landscape includes plantations and cultivated lands. Banana, pepper and ginger are among the crops grown in the region.

🏡 Administrative Villages

The three villages listed under Thavinhal Grama Panchayat are Periya, Thavinhal and Valad.

Natural Resources of Thavinhal

Major environmental and economic resources of the region

Forest Resources

Forest vegetation contributes to ecological stability, wildlife habitat, soil conservation and the maintenance of local environmental quality.

Water Resources

Streams and watercourses are important natural resources supporting agriculture, vegetation and the everyday needs of local communities.

Agricultural Resources

Agricultural land supports plantation and mixed-crop cultivation. Banana, pepper and ginger are important crops in the region.

Biodiversity Resources

The combination of forests, trees, streams and varied landscapes contributes to the ecological importance of the region.

Land and Landscape Resources

Hills, valleys and cultivated slopes form an important part of the physical landscape and provide opportunities for land resource studies.

Plantation and Vegetation

Green estates, plantations and tree vegetation contribute to the rural landscape and local agricultural economy.
